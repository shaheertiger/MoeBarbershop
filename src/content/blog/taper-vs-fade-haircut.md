"Taper" and "fade" get used like they mean the same thing, and that's how a lot
of guys end up with a haircut they didn't ask for. They're related, but they're
not the same cut.

**The short version:** a **taper** gradually shortens your hair at the
**sideburns and neckline**, usually leaving some length at the bottom. A
**fade** blends shorter **all the way around the sides and back**, often down to
bare skin. A taper is softer and grows out gracefully; a fade is sharper, with
more contrast, and needs touching up sooner. Every fade is technically a type of
taper — but not every taper is a fade.

## Taper vs. fade at a glance

| | Taper | Fade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it gets shorter | Sideburns and neckline | All around the sides and back |
| How short it goes | Short, usually not bare skin | Down to the skin, or close to it |
| Look | Soft, natural, classic | Sharp, clean, high-contrast |
| Grows out | Gradually, stays tidy longer | Growth shows within 1–3 weeks |
| Touch-ups | Every 3–4 weeks | Every 2–3 weeks |

## What is a taper?

A taper follows your natural hairline. The length steps down gradually as it
reaches the edges, so the sideburns and the back of the neck look clean and
finished without exposing much skin. From a distance it reads as a neat,
well-kept haircut rather than a "style".

Tapers are great if you:

- work somewhere with a conservative dress code, a uniform or a helmet;
- are growing your hair out and want it to look intentional along the way;
- don't want to see your barber every two weeks.

A **low taper** keeps the change right at the hairline. A **high taper** starts
the step-down higher on the sides for a more obviously short finish.

## What is a fade?

A fade takes the hair from longer on top down to very short — often bare skin —
around the entire sides and back, with a seamless blend in between. Where that
blend starts gives each fade its name:

- **Low fade** — starts just above the ears. Subtle and office-friendly.
- **Mid fade** — starts around the temples. Balanced on almost everyone.
- **High fade** — starts high on the sides for maximum contrast.
- **Skin (bald) fade** — any fade taken all the way down to the skin.
- **Burst and drop fades** — curve around or dip behind the ear.

Fades are the most-requested cut in our chair because they look sharp with
nearly any top: textured crops, curls, side parts and pompadours. For a deeper
look at fade heights and face shapes, read our
[2026 skin fade guide](/blog/skin-fade-guide-erin-ontario).

## The low taper fade: the best of both

The **low taper fade** is the cut people keep asking about. It's a hybrid: we
fade down to the skin **only at the sideburns and neckline**, and keep a tapered
length everywhere else on the sides. You get the crisp, clean edge of a fade
with the soft outline and slower grow-out of a taper.

It's a great middle ground if a full skin fade feels like too much but a
classic taper feels too plain.

## Which one should you get?

- **Want the most low-maintenance option?** Go with a taper. It still looks tidy
  at week four.
- **Want a sharp, modern look?** Go with a fade. Pick low or mid if you want to
  keep it subtle.
- **Strict dress code at work or school?** A low taper or a low fade.
- **Curly or coily hair?** Both work beautifully. Fades show off definition on
  top; tapers keep a fuller, rounder shape.
- **Wear a beard?** A fade or taper that blends into your beard connects the
  whole look — ask about the [Haircut & Beard combo](/services/haircut-and-beard).

## How to ask your barber for exactly what you want

The words you use matter. Try one of these:

- *"Low taper — keep the length on the sides, clean up the sideburns and
  neckline."*
- *"Mid skin fade, blended into a #2 on the sides, textured on top."*
- *"Low taper fade — skin at the sideburns and the back, keep the rest soft."*

If you mention clipper guard numbers, here's what they mean:

| Guard | Length left |
| --- | --- |
| #1 | 1/8 inch (about 3 mm) |
| #2 | 1/4 inch (about 6 mm) |
| #3 | 3/8 inch (about 10 mm) |
| #4 | 1/2 inch (about 13 mm) |

And tell your barber **how often you realistically get your hair cut**. If it's
every five weeks, a skin fade will spend half that time looking grown out — a
taper might serve you better. (Here's
[how often each style needs a cut](/blog/how-often-should-men-get-a-haircut).)

Bring a photo if you have one. It's the fastest way to get us on the same page,
and the consultation at the start of every appointment at Moe's is exactly the
time to sort it out.
