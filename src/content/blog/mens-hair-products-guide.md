You walked out of the barbershop looking sharp. Two days later, you can't get it
to sit the same way. Nine times out of ten, the issue isn't the cut — it's the
product (or how much of it you're using).

Here's the quick guide we give clients at Moe's Barbershop.

## The two questions that matter

Every hair product comes down to two things:

1. **Hold** — how firmly it keeps your hair in place (light, medium, strong)
2. **Shine** — how much it reflects light (matte, natural, high shine)

Choose those first, and the right product becomes obvious.

## The main products, explained

### Matte clay

**Hold:** medium to strong · **Shine:** matte

The most versatile product in the shop. Clay adds texture and thickness with a
natural, "nothing in my hair" look. Perfect for textured crops, messy styles and
thinning hair.

### Pomade

**Hold:** light to strong · **Shine:** medium to high

The classic choice for slick-backs, pompadours and side parts. Water-based
pomades wash out easily; oil-based ones offer more hold but take more effort to
remove.

### Wax

**Hold:** medium · **Shine:** low to medium

A flexible, re-workable hold that's great for spiky or piecey looks. You can
restyle it through the day.

### Paste

**Hold:** medium · **Shine:** low to medium

Somewhere between a clay and a pomade. A solid everyday option for short to
medium cuts.

### Cream

**Hold:** light · **Shine:** natural

Ideal for longer hair, curls and waves. Adds control and fights frizz without
making hair stiff. (See our [curly hair guide](/blog/curly-hair-mens-haircuts).)

### Sea salt spray

**Hold:** light · **Shine:** matte

A pre-styler that adds volume and grit. Spray into damp hair, blow-dry, then
finish with clay.

## Match the product to your cut

| Your cut | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Textured crop | Matte clay or paste |
| Skin fade with length on top | Clay or pomade |
| Side part / slick-back | Pomade |
| Curly or wavy top | Cream |
| Thinning hair | Matte clay or sea salt spray |
| Short crew cut | Light paste, or nothing at all |

## How to apply it properly

1. **Start small.** A fingertip-sized amount is plenty. You can always add more.
2. **Warm it up.** Rub it between your palms until it's almost invisible.
3. **Back to front.** Work it in from the back of your head forward so the front
   doesn't get overloaded.
4. **Roots first, then shape.** Get product down to the roots for support, then
   style the ends.
5. **Keep it off the sides of a fade.** Product on a fresh skin fade just looks
   greasy. Focus on the top.
