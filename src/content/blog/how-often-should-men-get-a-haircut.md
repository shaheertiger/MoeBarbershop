Most men look their best with a haircut **every 2 to 6 weeks** — but the right
number depends almost entirely on your style. The shorter the sides, the sooner
the growth shows:

- **Skin fades:** every 2–3 weeks
- **Classic short cuts and tapers:** every 3–5 weeks
- **Longer styles:** every 6–8 weeks
- **Beard lineups:** every 2–3 weeks

Here's why, plus a full schedule by style and a few tricks for stretching the
time between visits.

## Why short styles need cutting more often

Hair grows about **half an inch (roughly 1.25 cm) a month** on average. On
longer hair, a couple of weeks of growth barely registers. On a skin fade,
where the blend starts at bare skin, the same growth wipes out the contrast
that made the cut look sharp in the first place.

That's the rule of thumb: **the closer to the skin, the faster it grows out of
shape.**

## Haircut schedule by style

| Style | How often | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skin or high fade | Every 2–3 weeks | Growth near the skin shows first |
| Low fade or low taper fade | Every 3–4 weeks | A softer blend grows out more gracefully |
| Buzz cut | Every 2–4 weeks | Uniform length loses its crispness fast |
| Classic short cut, side part or taper | Every 3–5 weeks | Neckline and ears get fuzzy first |
| Textured crop, medium length | Every 4–6 weeks | Top keeps its shape; sides need a tidy |
| Longer hair or growing it out | Every 6–8 weeks | Shape-ups keep it looking intentional |
| Beard fade and lineup | Every 2–3 weeks | Cheek and neck lines soften quickly |
| Kids' cuts | Every 4–6 weeks | Keeps hair out of their eyes |

Not sure whether your cut counts as a fade or a taper? Our

## Signs you're overdue

- The neckline looks fuzzy, or hair is creeping down the back of your neck.
- Hair is touching or covering the tops of your ears.
- Your fade has lost its shadow — the sides look like one flat length.
- Styling takes twice as long, and cowlicks are starting to win.
- Your beard edge has blurred into your cheeks.

If you're nodding at two or more, it's time to book.

## Growing your hair out? You still need trims

It sounds backwards, but skipping the barber completely is the slowest way to
grow your hair out. Without shaping, the sides and back puff out long before the
top reaches the length you want — the awkward stage everyone dreads. A
shape-up every **6–8 weeks** keeps the sides and neckline tidy while the top
keeps its length, so it looks intentional the whole way.

## How to stretch the time between cuts

1. **Choose a style that grows out well.** A low fade or taper looks
   presentable for longer than a high skin fade.
2. **Use the right product.** A matte clay or light pomade on top hides the
   first week or two of growth far better than heavy gel.
3. **Keep the neck tidy — carefully.** Clean up stray hairs well below the
   neckline, but never try to move the line itself.
4. **Book your next cut before you leave.** It's the simplest way to stay in
   that sweet spot instead of drifting to week six.
