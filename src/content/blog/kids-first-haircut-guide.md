A first haircut is a real milestone — for your little one, and honestly, for
you too. It can also be a bit nerve-racking: new place, new person, a buzzing
clipper, and a cape they're not allowed to pull off. The good news is that with
a little planning, most first haircuts are quick, calm and something you'll want
photos of.

Here's what we tell parents at Moe's Barbershop in Erin.

## When should your child get their first haircut?

There's no "right" age. Many families book the first cut somewhere between the
**first and second birthday**, but plenty of kids go earlier or later. Better
signs than age:

- Hair is falling into their eyes.
- It tangles easily or gets matted at the back.
- It's growing unevenly — long at the back, wispy at the front.
- A family photo, holiday or special event is coming up.

Some families also time the first cut around a cultural or religious tradition.
That's wonderful — tell us what you have in mind and we'll work around it.

## Choose the right moment

Timing does more than anything else to make a first cut go smoothly:

- **Go when they're rested and fed.** Mid-morning, or right after a nap, beats
  the late-afternoon meltdown window.
- **Pick a calmer time.** At most barbershops, weekday mornings are quieter than
  Saturday afternoons. We open at 9:30 a.m. every day.
- **Don't squeeze it in.** Leave a little buffer so nobody feels rushed.

## How to prepare your child

1. **Talk about it beforehand.** Keep it upbeat and simple: "We're going to get
   your hair cut so it's nice and neat."
2. **Play barbershop at home.** A comb, a spray bottle and a pretend "haircut"
   on a favourite stuffed animal make the real thing feel familiar.
3. **Let them watch first.** If a parent or older sibling needs a cut, bring
   them along and let your child watch how easy it is.
4. **Bring a comfort item.** A favourite toy, a tablet with a show, or a
   (non-sticky) snack can work wonders.
5. **Dress for it.** A comfortable, loose shirt — and a spare one — helps with
   itchy little hairs.

## What happens in the chair

A kids' cut at Moe's takes about **20 minutes**, and we keep first cuts relaxed
and unhurried. Very young children often feel safest on a parent's lap — let us
know when you arrive and we'll set up whatever keeps them comfortable. You can
stay right beside them the whole time.

A few things that help:

- **Clippers can be loud.** If the buzz worries your child, we can show it to
  them first and let them feel it on their hand. Scissors are quieter, so a
  nervous first-timer may get more scissor work.
- **Short is fine.** A first cut doesn't need to be a big change. A tidy trim
  that takes hair out of their eyes is a great start.
- **Tears are normal.** If it happens, we pause, reset and carry on. Most kids
  settle quickly once they realize nothing hurts.

## Keep the memory

- **Save a lock of hair.** Many parents like to keep a small lock as a
  keepsake — just mention it before we start.
- **Take before-and-after photos.** You'll want them later, trust us.

## After the first cut

Give your child a quick rinse or bath afterward to get rid of itchy clippings.
For most short kids' styles, a trim **every 4–6 weeks** keeps hair neat and out
of their eyes — our guide to
[how often to get a haircut](/blog/how-often-should-men-get-a-haircut) has a
schedule for every style.

Once haircuts become routine, many parents book back-to-back so the whole family
is done in one trip — and grandparents are welcome too, with our
