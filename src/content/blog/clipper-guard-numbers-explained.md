**Quick answer:** a **#1 guard is 3 mm**, a **#1.5 is 4.5 mm**, a **#2 is 6 mm**,
a **#3 is 10 mm** and a **#4 is 13 mm**. Each whole number adds roughly 1/8 inch
(about 3 mm).

"Number two on the sides, please." It's one of the most common requests we hear
at Moe's Barbershop — and one of the most misunderstood. Clipper guard numbers
are handy shorthand, but only if you know what they actually leave behind.

## Clipper guard sizes chart (mm and inches)

| Guard | Length (mm) | Length (inches) | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #0 (no guard) | ~0.5–1.5 mm | ~1/16" | Very close; scalp clearly visible |
| #0.5 | 1.5 mm | 1/16" | Softens a fade just above the skin |
| #1 | 3 mm | 1/8" | Short buzz; scalp shows through |
| #1.5 | 4.5 mm | 3/16" | The go-to half size for blending fades |
| #2 | 6 mm | 1/4" | Short and neat; a little scalp shows |
| #2.5 | 8 mm | 5/16" | Blends a #2 into a #3 |
| #3 | 10 mm | 3/8" | Classic short sides; most popular buzz |
| #4 | 13 mm | 1/2" | Short but full; great first buzz |
| #5 | 16 mm | 5/8" | Soft, natural length |
| #6 | 19 mm | 3/4" | Longer sides, more coverage |
| #7 | 22 mm | 7/8" | Long clipper length |
| #8 | 25 mm | 1" | The longest standard guard |

Guard lengths vary slightly between clipper brands, so treat these as close
approximations rather than exact measurements.

## The most-asked sizes

- **#1 guard — 3 mm.** A very short buzz. Expect to see scalp, especially on
  lighter or finer hair.
- **#1.5 guard — 4.5 mm.** Barely longer than a #1, but enough to soften the
  line between a #1 and a #2. Most fades use it.
- **#2 guard — 6 mm.** The classic "short on the sides." Neat, with only a
  little scalp showing on darker hair.
- **#3 guard — 10 mm.** The most popular all-round length, for both short sides
  and a buzz cut all over.

## Half sizes and the clipper lever

Half guards (#0.5, #1.5, #2.5) exist to make blending smoother. If you cut at
home and don't own them, the **lever** on the side of most clippers does a
similar job: opening it adds roughly 1.5 mm. So:

- **#0 with the lever open** ≈ #0.5 (about 1.5 mm)
- **#1 with the lever open** ≈ #1.5 (about 4.5 mm)
- **#2 with the lever open** ≈ #2.5 (about 8 mm)

## Where a skin fade fits in

A [skin fade](/services/skin-fade) doesn't use one number — it uses several. We
start at the skin with a foil shaver or open blade, then work up through the
half sizes (#0.5, #1, #1.5, #2) to blend into the length on top. That gradual
step-up is what gives a fade its smooth, seamless look. Not sure if you want a
fade or a taper? Here's [the difference](/blog/taper-vs-fade-haircut).

## Darker vs. lighter hair

The same guard can look very different depending on your hair. On dark, thick
hair, a #2 still looks fairly full. On fine or light-coloured hair, a #2 can
show quite a bit of scalp. If you're not sure, go one number longer — we can
always take more off, but we can't put it back.

## Which guard for a buzz cut?

For a buzz all over, most people land between a **#2 and a #4**. A #3 is the
safe, popular choice; a #4 is the best first buzz. Our
[buzz cut guide](/blog/buzz-cut-guide) covers every length and style.

## How to ask for what you want

- **Sides and top:** "#2 on the sides, scissor-cut on top, about two inches."
- **A fade:** "Mid skin fade, blended into a #3, leave the top long."
- **A taper:** "Taper the neckline and sideburns, #3 on the sides."
- **A buzz:** "#4 all over, square up the neckline."

Bring a photo if you have one. Numbers plus a picture is the fastest way for
any barber to get it exactly right.
