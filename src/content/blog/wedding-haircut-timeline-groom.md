Your wedding photos will be around a lot longer than your haircut, so the timing
matters more than most grooms realize. Cut too close to the day and you risk
harsh lines, red skin or a mistake with no time to fix it. Cut too early and
you'll walk down the aisle looking two weeks overdue.

**The short answer:** get your final wedding haircut **2–5 days before** the
wedding, and your final beard lineup **1–3 days before**. If you're trying a new
style, do a **trial cut 6–8 weeks out**.

Here's the full countdown we give grooms at Moe's Barbershop in Erin.

## The wedding grooming countdown

| When | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8–12 weeks out | Choose your style; start growing a beard | Beards and longer styles need time |
| 6–8 weeks out | Trial cut in the exact style | Time to adjust if you don't love it |
| 3–4 weeks out | Maintenance cut; book the wedding-week cuts | Keeps the shape; weekend slots fill up |
| 2–5 days out | Final haircut | Fresh, with edges that have softened |
| 1–3 days out | Final beard lineup | Crisp lines, calm skin |
| Wedding morning | Style only — no new cuts or shaving | Nothing to go wrong |

## 8–12 weeks out: decide, and start growing

If you want a beard in the photos, now's the time to start growing it — most
beards need a couple of months to fill in evenly. The same goes for a longer
style on top: stop cutting the length now, but keep the sides and neckline tidy
so it looks intentional as it grows.

## 6–8 weeks out: the trial cut

Planning something new? This is your dress rehearsal. Bring photos of the look
you want — and ideally of your suit or tux — and get the **exact** cut you plan
to wear on the day. Take a few photos of it, then see how it looks after a week
or two of growth. If it's not right, there's still time to adjust or grow it
back.

Want the full treatment for your trial? Our signature **Moe's Haircut** is a
full hour with an unhurried consultation, a precision cut, detailed finishing
and a hot-towel finish.

## 3–4 weeks out: maintenance cut and booking

Get a maintenance cut to keep your shape on track. If your style is on the
longer side, this is usually the cut that sets the length you'll wear on the
day.

This is also the moment to **book your wedding-week appointments** — for you
and for your wedding party. Weekend slots fill up quickly in wedding season.

## 2–5 days out: the final haircut

This is the one that matters. A cut two to five days before the wedding looks
fresh in the photos, but gives any harsh edges a chance to soften and any skin
irritation from edge work time to calm down.

- **Skin fades:** book 2–3 days out. Fades look sharpest in their first few
  days. Not sure a fade is right for the day? See our
  [taper vs. fade guide](/blog/taper-vs-fade-haircut).
- **Classic cuts and tapers:** 3–5 days out.
- **Longer styles:** up to a week out is fine.

## 1–3 days out: the beard

Get your final beard shape and lineup a day or three before the wedding. The
lines will be crisp, and your skin will have settled by the time the camera
comes out. The easy option is the [Haircut & Beard combo](/services/haircut-and-beard)
three days out, which covers both in one 30-minute visit. If you prefer a
separate appointment, book a [Beard Fade & Lineup](/services/beard-trim-and-lineup)
a day or two before.

Between now and the wedding, keep your hands off the lines — our
[beard grooming guide](/blog/beard-grooming-tips) explains why.

## The wedding morning

**Don't shave, trim or edge anything yourself.** A razor bump or a crooked
neckline is the last thing you want in close-up photos. Wash, apply your usual
product the way you practised after the trial, and bring a small comb and a bit
of product for touch-ups between the ceremony and the reception.

## Getting the groomsmen on the same page

Everyone in the photos should look like they're from the same wedding:

- **Same window.** Have the groomsmen get cut in the same 2–5-day window as the
  groom.

- **Don't forget the rest of the party.** The ring bearer and page boy can book

## Wedding-week tips

- **No new products.** Wedding week isn't the time to experiment.
- **Mind the season.** For an outdoor summer wedding, shorter sides keep you
  cooler; in winter, a touch more length can look fuller.
- **Travelling for the wedding?** Get your final cut before you leave rather
  than gambling on an unfamiliar shop.
- **Tell your barber the date.** We'll plan every cut around it.
